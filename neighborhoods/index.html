Neighborhoods

Chicago, neighborhood by neighborhood.

These are the neighborhoods where I work most often. Each has its own housing stock, and its own questions to ask before you buy.

  • 01

    Lincoln Park

    Victorian greystones, brick row houses and vintage courtyard buildings around the park and the lakefront.

    Worth asking: Parts of the neighborhood sit in the Mid-North landmark district, where exterior changes go through city landmark review. In vintage condo buildings, ask about the age of the roof, masonry and mechanical systems.

  • 02

    Lakeview

    Two- and three-flats, vintage courtyard buildings and lakefront high-rises, from Wrigleyville to East Lakeview.

    Worth asking: Many condos here are in small, self-managed associations. Ask how reserves are funded, who handles maintenance, and whether a big repair would mean a special assessment.

  • 03

    Gold Coast

    Mansions and townhouses in the Astor Street landmark district, and prewar co-ops and condos facing the lake.

    Worth asking: Co-ops work differently from condos. Ask about board approval, financing limits, and whether the building carries its own mortgage.

  • 04

    Old Town

    Nineteenth-century workers' cottages and Victorian homes in the Old Town Triangle, close to downtown.

    Worth asking: Homes in the Old Town Triangle landmark district need city review for exterior work. Factor that into any renovation plans.

  • 05

    Streeterville

    Lakefront towers, including Mies van der Rohe's 860–880 North Lake Shore Drive, steps from Michigan Avenue.

    Worth asking: In high-rises, ask about façade inspection and repair schedules, elevator work, and whether parking is deeded, leased or not included.

  • 06

    West Loop

    Converted warehouse lofts beside new construction, around the restaurant corridor of Fulton Market.

    Worth asking: Lofts vary a lot. Ask about construction (timber or concrete) and sound between units, and look at what's planned on neighboring lots.

  • 07

    Ravenswood

    Tree-lined streets of single-family homes and two-flats along the Metra line.

    Worth asking: For houses and two-flats, ask about basement water history, sewer line condition, and the age of major systems.

  • 08

    River North

    Brick loft conversions, Bertrand Goldberg's Marina City and newer towers near the river.

    Worth asking: Ask what share of units are rented, and what the association's rules say about leasing and short-term rentals.

  • 09

    South Loop

    Printer's Row lofts, the historic Prairie Avenue district and newer high-rises near the Museum Campus.

    Worth asking: In newer towers, ask when the association took over from the developer and how reserves were funded at that handoff.
