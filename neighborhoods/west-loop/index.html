Neighborhoods / West Loop

West Loop

Converted warehouses, new towers and Chicago's busiest restaurant scene, just across the river from the Loop.

01

The neighborhood

The West Loop was a district of meatpackers, produce markets and factories. Today Fulton Market and Randolph Street hold many of the city's most talked-about restaurants, alongside offices for companies that moved here, including Google.

Mary Bartelme and Skinner parks give the residential blocks some green space.

02

Homes you'll find

The housing you'll see most often in West Loop.

  • Loft conversionsTimber and concrete lofts in former warehouses and factories.
  • New-construction condosMid-rise and high-rise buildings, many built in the last decade.
  • TownhomesNewer row homes, often with garages and roof decks.
  • Older homesA smaller number of greystones and single-family houses.
03

Getting around

  • Green and Pink LinesMorgan and Clinton stations.
  • Blue LineUIC-Halsted and Clinton stations.
  • MetraUnion Station and Ogilvie Transportation Center.
  • DrivingQuick access to the Kennedy and Eisenhower expressways.
04

Worth a look

Architecture and landmarks I point out on tours.

  • Fulton-Randolph Market District

    A landmark district protecting the area's market buildings.

  • Union Station

    Its 1925 Great Hall is one of the city's great interiors.

  • Old St. Patrick's Church

    Dedicated in 1856, it survived the Great Chicago Fire.

05

What I look at before you buy

Lofts vary a lot. A true timber-loft conversion and a newer "soft loft" can look similar in photos and age very differently. I look at the conversion date, the building's mechanical systems and how the association has planned for big-ticket items.

With so much construction nearby, I also check what's planned on neighboring lots, since a new tower can change a view.
