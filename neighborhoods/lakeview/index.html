Neighborhoods / Lakeview

Lakeview, Chicago

Big, lively and varied, from Wrigley Field to the lakefront, with vintage buildings on nearly every block.

Housing
Condos of every era, two- and three-flats, and single-family homes west of Clark
Typical buildings
Vintage courtyard buildings, greystones, newer three-unit duplex buildings, lakefront high-rises
Often a fit for
Buyers who want walkability, transit and a lively street life, often for a first or second home
Compare with
Lincoln Park, Ravenswood, Uptown
01

The neighborhood

Lakeview is really several neighborhoods in one: Lakeview East along the lake, Wrigleyville around the ballpark, the Southport Corridor with its shops and restaurants, and Northalsted, the historic center of Chicago's LGBTQ+ community.

It's dense, walkable and well served by transit, with Belmont Harbor and the lakefront on its eastern edge.

02

Lakeview, section by section

How the neighborhood changes from one part to the next.

  • Lakeview EastBetween the lake and roughly Halsted: high-rise condos and co-ops near Belmont Harbor, vintage courtyard buildings, and Northalsted's commercial strip.
  • WrigleyvilleThe blocks around Clark and Addison. Condos and rentals, bars, and game-day crowds from spring into fall.
  • Southport CorridorSouthport Avenue from Belmont toward Irving Park: boutiques and restaurants, single-family homes and newer construction, and more families.
  • West LakeviewWest of Ashland toward the river: quieter residential blocks of frame houses, two-flats and newer single-family homes.
03

Homes you'll find

The housing you'll see most often in Lakeview.

  • Vintage courtyard buildingsSome of the best-known examples of a classic Chicago building type.
  • Greystones, two-flats and three-flatsCommon throughout, either as condos or owner-occupied income properties.
  • Single-family homesFrame and brick houses, mostly west of Clark Street.
  • Newer duplex condosDuplex-up and duplex-down units in newer three-unit buildings.
  • Lakefront high-risesCondos and co-ops near Belmont Harbor and along Lake Shore Drive.
04

Buildings worth knowing

Architecture and landmarks I point out on tours.

  • Alta Vista Terrace

    A single block of early-1900s rowhouses, and one of Chicago's first landmark districts.

  • Wrigley Field

    The ballpark has anchored the neighborhood since 1914.

  • Belmont Harbor

    One of the busiest harbors on the city's lakefront.

05

Real estate notes

What's specific about buying and owning in Lakeview.

  • Many newer condos are duplex units in three-unit buildings with no management company. The builder's track record and any remaining warranty matter.
  • Near Wrigley Field, game days change parking, noise and traffic. Check the block on a game night before committing.
  • Courtyard buildings vary widely in upkeep. Masonry, roofs and back porches are the common big-ticket items.

How it compares

If you're also looking at…

  • Lincoln ParkMore single-family homes and more of the park.
  • RavenswoodQuieter and more residential, with more single-family homes and the Metra.
  • UptownMore vintage condos on the lakefront, with a growing number of new buildings near the rebuilt Red Line stations.
07

Getting around

  • Red LineBelmont, Addison and Sheridan stations.
  • Brown LineSouthport, Paulina, Belmont, Wellington and Diversey stations, with the Purple Line express at rush hour.
  • LakefrontBelmont Harbor, the Lakefront Trail and the lakefront parks.
