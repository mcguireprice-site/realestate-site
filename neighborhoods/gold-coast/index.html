Neighborhoods / Gold Coast

Gold Coast

Some of Chicago's grandest houses and most storied lakefront buildings, a short walk from Michigan Avenue.

01

The neighborhood

The Gold Coast took shape in the 1880s, after Potter Palmer built his mansion on the lakefront and other wealthy Chicagoans followed.

Today its quiet side streets of mansions and rowhouses sit next to Oak Street's boutiques, Rush Street's restaurants and Oak Street Beach.

02

Homes you'll find

The housing you'll see most often in Gold Coast.

  • Mansions and rowhousesMany on Astor Street and the surrounding blocks, some divided into condos.
  • Prewar co-opsElegant apartment buildings along East Lake Shore Drive and the lakefront.
  • Vintage mid-rise condosSmaller buildings on the side streets.
  • Modern high-risesFull-service towers with lake and city views.
03

Getting around

  • Red LineClark/Division station.
  • BusesFrequent service along Michigan Avenue and Lake Shore Drive.
  • On footEasy walks to Streeterville, Old Town and Michigan Avenue.
04

Worth a look

Architecture and landmarks I point out on tours.

  • Astor Street District

    A landmark district of late-1800s and early-1900s homes.

  • Charnley-Persky House

    Designed in 1892 by Louis Sullivan with a young Frank Lloyd Wright, and a National Historic Landmark.

  • East Lake Shore Drive District

    A landmark row of 1910s and 1920s apartment buildings facing the lake.

  • Archbishop's Residence

    An 1880s mansion known for its many chimneys.

05

What I look at before you buy

Co-ops are common here, especially in the prewar buildings. A co-op purchase involves a board application, building-specific financing rules and sometimes a larger down payment, so I confirm all of that before we get serious about a unit.

In older towers, I also ask about facade work, since Chicago requires regular inspections of high-rise exteriors.
