Neighborhoods / Gold Coast

Gold Coast, Chicago

Some of Chicago's grandest houses and most storied lakefront buildings, a short walk from Michigan Avenue.

Housing
Mostly high-rise condos and co-ops, plus historic single-family homes and rowhouses
Typical buildings
Prewar co-ops, 1960s and 1970s towers, Gilded Age houses, newer luxury towers
Often a fit for
Buyers who want to walk everywhere, prewar character, or a quiet street close to downtown
Compare with
Streeterville, Old Town, Lincoln Park
01

The neighborhood

The Gold Coast took shape in the 1880s, after Potter Palmer built his mansion on the lakefront and other wealthy Chicagoans followed.

Today its quiet side streets of mansions and rowhouses sit next to Oak Street's boutiques, Rush Street's restaurants and Oak Street Beach.

02

Gold Coast, section by section

How the neighborhood changes from one part to the next.

  • East Lake Shore Drive and the lakefrontA short, landmarked row of 1910s and 1920s apartment buildings facing Oak Street Beach. Many are co-ops with large prewar floor plans and board approval.
  • Astor Street and the historic coreAstor, State Parkway and Dearborn between Division and North Avenue hold the mansions, rowhouses and smaller vintage buildings. The quietest part of the neighborhood, and much of it is in the Astor Street landmark district.
  • Rush, Oak and DivisionThe commercial edge: boutiques, restaurants and nightlife, with high-rise condos above. More convenience, more noise.
  • West of ClarkCarl Sandburg Village and other mid-century towers. Generally the most accessible prices in the Gold Coast, with a different feel from the historic blocks east of Clark.
03

Homes you'll find

The housing you'll see most often in Gold Coast.

  • Mansions and rowhousesMany on Astor Street and the surrounding blocks, some divided into condos.
  • Prewar co-opsElegant apartment buildings along East Lake Shore Drive and the lakefront.
  • Vintage mid-rise condosSmaller buildings on the side streets.
  • Modern high-risesFull-service towers with lake and city views.
04

Buildings worth knowing

Architecture and landmarks I point out on tours.

  • Astor Street District

    A landmark district of late-1800s and early-1900s homes.

  • Charnley-Persky House

    Designed in 1892 by Louis Sullivan with a young Frank Lloyd Wright, and a National Historic Landmark.

  • East Lake Shore Drive District

    A landmark row of 1910s and 1920s apartment buildings facing the lake.

  • Archbishop's Residence

    An 1880s mansion known for its many chimneys.

05

Real estate notes

What's specific about buying and owning in Gold Coast.

  • Co-ops are common in the prewar buildings. Expect a board application, building-specific financing rules and often a larger down payment than for a condo.
  • Exterior changes on landmark-district homes need review by the Commission on Chicago Landmarks.
  • Older towers fall under Chicago's high-rise facade inspection rules. Ask about recent and upcoming facade work and how it is being paid for.
  • Parking is limited in the historic core. Whether a space is deeded, leased or unavailable can change a unit's value.

06

How it compares

If you're also looking at…

  • StreetervilleNewer towers and more amenities, fewer historic side streets. Fewer co-ops, so financing is usually simpler.
  • Old TownSmaller-scale: rowhouses, cottages and mid-rise condos, with more of a neighborhood feel.
  • Lincoln ParkMore single-family homes and space, the park at the door, and a longer trip downtown.
07

Getting around

  • BusesFrequent service along Michigan Avenue and Lake Shore Drive.
