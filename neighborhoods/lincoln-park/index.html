Neighborhoods / Lincoln Park

Lincoln Park, Chicago

Tree-lined streets, the park and the lakefront, and some of the city's best-kept nineteenth-century homes.

Housing
Single-family homes, greystones and vintage condos, plus lakefront high-rises
Typical buildings
Post-fire rowhouses and Victorians, two- and three-flats, courtyard buildings, 1960s and 1970s towers
Often a fit for
Buyers who want a house in the city, park access, or a quieter block close to everything
Compare with
Lakeview, Old Town, Gold Coast
01

The neighborhood

Lincoln Park takes its name from the park that runs along the lake, home to the free Lincoln Park Zoo, the Conservatory and miles of lakefront path.

West of the park, quiet residential blocks meet busy commercial streets like Armitage, Halsted and Lincoln, and DePaul University's campus anchors the neighborhood's western side.

02

Lincoln Park, section by section

How the neighborhood changes from one part to the next.

  • Park West and the lakefrontEast of Clark, along Lincoln Park West and Lakeview Avenue: high-rise condos and co-ops facing the park, and some of the neighborhood's grandest vintage buildings on the side streets.
  • The landmark coreThe Mid-North and Arlington-Deming districts hold blocks of rowhouses and single-family homes from the decades after the 1871 fire. Quiet, and protected from teardowns.
  • DePaul and SheffieldGreystones, two-flats and single-family homes around the campus. The blocks closest to DePaul have more student rentals and more evening foot traffic.
  • West of HalstedToward the river, in Ranch Triangle and along Clybourn: newer single-family homes and townhomes on standard city lots, with more parking and newer systems.
03

Homes you'll find

The housing you'll see most often in Lincoln Park.

  • Greystones and brick rowhousesMany date to the decades after the 1871 fire, often converted to condos or restored as single-family homes.
  • Victorian single-family homesConcentrated in the Mid-North and Sheffield areas, some on landmark-protected blocks.
  • Vintage courtyard and walk-up condosOften in small, self-managed associations.
  • Lakefront high-risesCondos and co-ops along Lincoln Park West and Lakeview Avenue, many with park views.
04

Buildings worth knowing

Architecture and landmarks I point out on tours.

  • Mid-North District

    A landmark district of homes built in the years after the Great Chicago Fire.

  • Lincoln Park Conservatory

    A Victorian-era glass house, open to the public year-round.

  • Alfred Caldwell Lily Pool

    A Prairie School landscape tucked inside the park, and a National Historic Landmark.

  • Armitage-Halsted District

    A landmark commercial district of nineteenth-century storefronts.

05

Real estate notes

What's specific about buying and owning in Lincoln Park.

  • Many vintage condos sit in small associations of three to six units. Dues can be low, but so can reserves, and decisions rest on a few neighbors.
  • Parts of the neighborhood are in landmark districts, where exterior changes need city review.
  • Public school attendance boundaries drive demand on some blocks. Check any address with Chicago Public Schools' school locator.

06

How it compares

If you're also looking at…

  • LakeviewDenser and busier, with more duplex condos and rentals. Lincoln Park has more single-family homes and more of the park.
  • Old TownSmaller-scale and closer to downtown, with cottages and rowhouses. Lincoln Park has more space and more houses.
  • Gold CoastMore high-rise and more formal. Lincoln Park trades some of that polish for yards, side streets and the park.
07

Getting around

  • Brown and Purple LinesArmitage, Fullerton and Diversey stations. The Purple Line runs express at rush hour.
  • Red LineFullerton and North/Clybourn stations.
  • LakefrontThe Lakefront Trail and North Avenue Beach are close by.
