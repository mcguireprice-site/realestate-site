Neighborhoods / Old Town

Old Town, Chicago

Some of the city's oldest residential blocks, with cottages and rowhouses a short walk from downtown.

Housing
Rowhouses, cottages and single-family homes, with condos in mid-rises and newer buildings
Typical buildings
Workers' cottages, Italianate and Queen Anne rowhouses, vintage walk-ups, newer townhomes
Often a fit for
Buyers who want an older, small-scale neighborhood within walking distance of downtown
Compare with
Gold Coast, Lincoln Park
01

The neighborhood

Old Town grew around a community of German immigrants in the mid-1800s. Wells Street is the main strip and home to The Second City.

The Old Town Triangle holds narrow streets of carefully kept historic homes, and each June the Old Town Art Fair fills them.

02

Old Town, section by section

How the neighborhood changes from one part to the next.

  • The Old Town TriangleNorth of North Avenue, around streets like Menomonee and Eugenie: narrow, winding blocks of nineteenth-century homes inside a landmark district.
  • Wells StreetThe commercial spine, with The Second City, restaurants and shops, and condos above storefronts and in mid-rises nearby.
  • Around Division and LarrabeeNewer townhomes and mid-rise buildings built over the past two decades as the area was redeveloped.
03

Homes you'll find

The housing you'll see most often in Old Town.

  • Workers' cottagesSmall frame and brick houses, many expanded and restored.
  • Italianate and Queen Anne rowhousesConcentrated in the Old Town Triangle.
  • Vintage and loft condosIn smaller buildings on and around Wells Street.
  • Mid-rise and high-rise condosMostly near North Avenue and Sedgwick.
04

Buildings worth knowing

Architecture and landmarks I point out on tours.

  • Old Town Triangle District

    A landmark district of nineteenth-century homes and narrow, winding streets.

  • St. Michael's Church

    Its walls survived the Great Chicago Fire of 1871, and the church was rebuilt within them.

  • Crilly Court

    A short, private-feeling street of 1880s rowhouses and apartments.

05

Real estate notes

What's specific about buying and owning in Old Town.

  • Much of the Triangle is a landmark district, so additions, windows and other exterior changes need city review.
  • In older cottages, check foundations, the age of the systems and whether past work was permitted.
  • Street parking is tight. A garage or deeded space adds real value here.

How it compares

If you're also looking at…

  • Gold CoastGrander and more high-rise, with more co-ops. Old Town is lower and more residential.
  • Lincoln ParkMore space, more single-family homes and the park, a bit farther from downtown.
