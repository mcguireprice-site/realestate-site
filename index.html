02

What I look at that a listing won't show you

Photos and square footage are the easy part. These are the questions I work through with every client, whether the home is a first condo or a landmark.

  • The building

    Age, construction, mechanical systems, and the maintenance decisions the owners have made or put off.

  • The association

    What the monthly dues actually cover, how healthy the reserves are, and whether special assessments or rental caps are coming.

  • The block

    What's been built nearby, what's planned, and how the street feels at 8 a.m. and at 8 p.m.

  • The design

    Layout, light, materials and architectural character, the qualities that hold value and are hard to add later.

  • The long view

    Who is likely to buy this home from you someday, and what they'll be looking for.

  • The fit

    Your commute, your budget, your next five years. The best home on paper isn't always the right one for you.

03

Chicago, neighborhood by neighborhood

I work across the city, most often in these neighborhoods. Each has its own housing stock, and its own questions to ask before you buy.

  1. 01

    Lincoln Park

    Victorian greystones, brick row houses and vintage courtyard buildings around the park and the lakefront.

  2. 02

    Lakeview

    Two- and three-flats, vintage courtyard buildings and lakefront high-rises, from Wrigleyville to East Lakeview.

  3. 03

    Gold Coast

    Mansions and townhouses along Astor Street, and prewar co-ops facing the lake.

  4. 04

    Old Town

    Nineteenth-century workers' cottages and Victorian homes in the Old Town Triangle, close to downtown.

  5. 05

    Streeterville

    Lakefront towers, including Mies van der Rohe's 860–880 North Lake Shore Drive, steps from Michigan Avenue.

  6. 06

    West Loop

    Converted warehouse lofts beside new construction, in the restaurant corridor around Fulton Market.

  7. 07

    Ravenswood

    Tree-lined streets of single-family homes and two-flats along the Metra line.

  8. 08

    River North

    Brick loft conversions, Bertrand Goldberg's Marina City and newer towers near the river.

  9. 09

    South Loop

    Printer's Row lofts, the historic Prairie Avenue district and newer high-rises near the Museum Campus.

04  /  About

A broker who reads the details

I'm a broker with @properties Christie's International Real Estate, working with Team Caroline Chicago. I'm especially drawn to architecture and design: historic homes, modernist buildings and the one-of-a-kind properties that don't fit neatly into a search filter.

Most of my clients aren't buying anything unusual, though. They're buying a first condo, selling a two-flat, or trying to decide whether Lakeview or Ravenswood makes more sense for the next five years. My approach is the same either way: look closely, ask the questions people tend to skip, and give you a straight answer, even when the answer is to wait.

I'd rather be the person you call with a question two years before you move than the one who shows up only at the closing table.
