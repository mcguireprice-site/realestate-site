Sell

Selling in Chicago: a price you can defend.

Comparable sales tell you where the market has been. Your price has to make a case to the buyer most likely to want your home. Here's how I build it.

01

Pricing is an argument, not a number.

Two homes with the same square footage can sell for very different prices. The difference is usually in things a spreadsheet doesn't capture well.

I start with recent comparable sales, then adjust for what actually makes your home different. You'll get a suggested range and the reasoning behind it.

What changes the number

  • Floor planA layout that works with real furniture beats extra square feet that don't.
  • Light and exposureWhich way you face, what's across the street, and whether that view is protected.
  • ConditionWhat a buyer will budget to fix, which is often more than it actually costs.
  • Parking and outdoor spaceDeeded parking and usable outdoor space are scarce in most of the city, and buyers price them that way.
  • The buildingDues, reserves and upcoming work. A healthy association reads as value; a thin one reads as risk.
  • The competitionWhat else a buyer will see the same weekend, in your building and nearby.
  • The buyerWho is most likely to want this home, and what they'll compare it with.

02

Preparation

What to do before the first showing.

  • Repairs worth makingThe fixes buyers notice or their inspectors will flag. Not every project pays for itself, and I'll tell you which ones are worth it.
  • PresentationDecluttering, light and furniture placement so the home photographs and shows well.
  • Condo documentsGetting the association's disclosure documents requested early, so buyers' questions don't slow down the deal.
  • TimingWhen to list, given the market, your next move and the building's own calendar.
03

Marketing

Present the home to the buyers most likely to value it.

I write the listing around what makes the home different: the architecture, the building, the block. The right buyers find it and understand why it's priced the way it is.
